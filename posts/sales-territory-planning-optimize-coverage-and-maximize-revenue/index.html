Sales Territory Planning: Optimize Coverage and Maximize Revenue

2026-06-15 · Strategy · Tags: Territory Planning, Sales Coverage, Account Planning, Sales Strategy
Territory planning is one of the most impactful strategic activities a sales leader can undertake. Well-designed territories can increase revenue by 10-20% without adding headcount. ## Why Territory Planning Matters Poor territory design leads to: - Uneven workload distribution - Missed opportunities - Rep burnout and turnover - Inaccurate forecasting - Customer coverage gaps ## Territory Design Principles ### 1. Balance Opportunity Each territory should have similar revenue potential. Use: - Total addressable market (TAM) data - Historical revenue by region - Account tier distribution - Growth potential scores ### 2. Balance Workload Consider: - Number of accounts - Account complexity - Travel time and distance - Administrative requirements - Support obligations ### 3. Align with Customer Needs - Industry verticals - Company size segments - Buying behavior patterns - Time zone considerations - Language and cultural fit ## Territory Design Models ### Geographic Territories Divide by physical location. Simple but may not balance opportunity. **Best for:** Field sales, physical products, service areas. ### Account-Based Territories Assign specific accounts based on size, industry, or strategic importance. **Best for:** Enterprise sales, key account management. ### Hybrid Territories Combine geographic and account-based approaches. **Best for:** Most B2B organizations. ### Vertical Territories Organize by industry or market segment. **Best for:** Specialized solutions, industry-specific products. ## The Territory Planning Process ### Step 1: Data Collection Gather: - Current account list with revenue - Historical sales data - Market potential data - Rep performance data - Customer satisfaction scores ### Step 2: Analysis Use data to identify: - Underpenetrated accounts - Over-served accounts - Growth opportunities - Coverage gaps - Rep capacity ### Step 3: Design Create territory scenarios: - Model 2-3 different designs - Calculate coverage metrics for each - Get input from field reps - Refine based on feedback ### Step 4: Assignment Match reps to territories: - Rep skills and experience - Relationship continuity - Career development goals - Personal preferences ### Step 5: Implementation Roll out new territories: - Communicate rationale clearly - Provide transition support - Set up new reporting - Monitor for 90 days ## Conclusion Territory planning is not a one-time event. Review and adjust territories annually, or when significant changes occur.
